# Reproducible M6 human review protocol

Use the current isolated loopback demo. This is a protocol, not a completed user study. Human participants observed: **0**. Automated Chromium scripts are engineering checks only. The separate original-art reconstruction gate remains blocked.

## Setup

Record viewport, browser, OS/device, input method, assistive technology and reduced-motion preference. Use a new disposable fixture actor through the demo bootstrap. Do not use a real learner account, configured database or paid provider. Explain that the sources and qualification records are synthetic engineering fixtures and that the original artwork currently supports static poses only. Preserve recordings only with participant consent.

Use the approved M6 plan’s learner/teacher review cohort; record actual participant counts and roles, never infer them from scripted runs. Include desktop mouse, keyboard-only, 360px mobile/touch, an actual mobile keyboard, reduced motion, text-only and a screen-reader case. Emulation does not qualify physical mobile/assistive devices.

Recruit at least twelve representative learners across Cloud and English, plus the relevant teacher reviewers, before treating this protocol as a completed acceptance exercise. Record the actual coverage and exclusions; no participants have been recruited or observed by this task.

## Tasks and observable outcomes

1. Open Cloud, explain which work area Petros means during an accepted source highlight, and identify when it becomes your turn. Record wrong target interpretations and missed invitations.
2. Give an initial answer, inspect actual incorrect/partial feedback, open the teaching experiment, predict another VM, allocate to the real capacity boundary, explain it and submit an assisted correction. Ask the participant to distinguish exploration from a grade and assisted success from independent mastery.
3. Observe naturally pending evaluation and an English review-required result. Ask what is confirmed, what is still pending, and what Petros is actually checking. Never delay or forge the evaluator for the study.
4. Interrupt a source explanation with Ask Petros. Type/drag, pause, resume and reload. Record unwanted gestures, lost drafts, repeated reactions and whether the accepted canvas survives.
5. Edit an English evidence/claim connection and annotation, submit, and identify why qualified review remains required. Record whether highlighting refers to the actual current element.
6. Dismiss/reopen a caption, open its full transcript, minimize the character, enable reduced motion and continue in text-only mode. Verify keyboard focus and that answer fields and controls stay visible.
7. On a real phone, repeat prediction/correction and English editing with the keyboard open, then rotate/resize. Check input focus, scrolling, pause/resume and text alternatives. Compare with the recorded emulated 360px geometry.
8. Let the participant work for about ten minutes. Record repetitive speech/gestures, distraction, quiet waiting, unnecessary movement, response delays and their explicit preference for motion versus text.

After each task ask: “What was Petros referring to?”, “Whose turn was it?”, “Was this saved, checked, correct, partial, pending or review-required?”, and “Was the gesture helpful or distracting?” Use neutral wording. Record errors and quotes with consent, completion time and assistance given. Do not convert engagement or preference into learning evidence.

## Acceptance record

Keep raw observations, device-specific failures and participant counts. Distinguish functional safety, visual fidelity, accessibility and learner preference. Apply the approved plan’s human acceptance thresholds only to actual collected observations; do not invent a passing percentage. Any confusing correctness signal, obstructed input, lost work or ineffective interruption requires repair/retest. Human art approval must identify exact asset/clip hashes.
