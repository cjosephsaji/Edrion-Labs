## Setup and facilitation

Recruit students who have not seen this interface. Use authorized, reviewed activities appropriate for their course and the student's own account, or a clearly identified synthetic study account. Explain the observation/recording arrangements and obtain consent. Do not collect passwords or unnecessary personal information. Use participant codes instead of names in this sheet. Include keyboard-only users and assistive-technology users in the planned sample; browser emulation alone cannot represent them.

Use the production candidate after the mobile reader capture is resolved. Record actual build, browser, viewport, physical device, input method, text zoom, motion preference, course/source and account setup. If a supported task is unavailable for the reviewed course, mark it unavailable; do not substitute a fabricated validator or answer.

Read tasks without teaching the interface. Ask the student to think aloud if comfortable. Do not point to controls. Record requests for help and any assistance given. Stop a task if the participant wants to stop. Keep task success separate from academic correctness.

## Neutral tasks

| Task read to participant | Observe without prompting |
|---|---|
| Look at this lesson. Tell me what it is asking you to do and what you would do first. | Question comprehension; objective; whether the principal action is found |
| Make a prediction, then use the activity to test it. Continue until you can explain what the model allows. | Per-attempt predictions; committed versus selected state; main action; resource model |
| Explain what happened when another VM could not be added. | Refusal meaning; actual remaining/requested values; whether refusal is mistaken for a grade |
| Find help from Petros and read the full explanation. Return to your work. | Ask discoverability; short cloud; reader; scrolling; references; close/focus |
| Write your explanation and save it. Tell me what the interface now says about your work. | Draft retention; save action; saved versus checked understanding |
| Open the supported equation activity. Use the available reviewed operations to work through it, then save your answer. | Equation legibility; accepted operation versus selection; retained input; units/source limitations |
| Read the reviewed English source. Choose supporting evidence and explain the relationship to the claim. | Source access; locator/evidence; keyboard/form alternative; reasoning input |
| Submit a reviewed response when you are ready. Explain the feedback you receive. | Pending versus evaluated; teacher-review abstention; no fabricated certainty |
| Pause the session, return, and continue from your saved work. | Recovery; no lost draft/progress; expected companion behavior |
| Use the same lesson with your preferred mobile or keyboard setup. | Clouds/input priority; scrolling; primary controls; pointer alternatives; reading comfort |

Use the existing reviewed capacity-calculation workflow if that is the available mathematics activity. A standalone algebra lesson must be source-reviewed and supported before it is used in the study. English evidence may be represented by the existing argument diagram; do not imply text-span selection exists.

## Comprehension checks after the tasks

Ask in the student's own words: What does “Saved · Not checked yet” mean? What would tell you that evaluation had completed? What does “Teacher review needed” mean? Does a refused VM request say your explanation is wrong? Where did Petros's full explanation come from? Can an assisted correction replace the original independent answer?

Record each response verbatim where consent permits. Do not coach until recording the initial response. Then clarify any misunderstanding to avoid leaving the learner with an incorrect interpretation.

## Analysis and release decision

Record participants and sessions actually completed, completion times, assistance counts, comprehension responses, recurrent blockers, and differences across devices/input methods. Report unavailable tasks and missing cohorts. Avoid claims of statistical validity from a small convenience sample. Re-test concrete issues after fixes.
